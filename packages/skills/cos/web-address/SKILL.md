# Web address

The website's own address: a domain they have, finding or buying one, and connecting it. It is a task you lead.

## When to use
- They mention a domain, their web address or publishing on their own name.
- The address card was answered, Connect was pressed, or the address task is under way.

## Procedure
1. The open items note says where the address stands, its next step and when to bring it up; code keeps that record, so go by it and never offer the address on your own. The workspace's tasks hold its steps, a finished one included.
2. While an address task is under way, speak of its steps and offer nothing new.
3. When you bring it up, give the benefit in their terms (customers trust an address of their own, and it looks right on their quotes and cards) and that setting it up now means the website goes live on it the moment they publish, then ask whether they already have one.
4. Take the name from business.email in the facts, unless it is a free-mail address (gmail.com, outlook.com, yahoo.com, icloud.com and the like): then ask whether they have a domain.
5. When they have a domain but say the setting will have to wait (no login to hand, on their phone, later), put nothing off: the domain stays saved. Say so in your own words, with the why: their website goes live on its free address first so they can start sharing it, and the setting waits in their panel for whenever they're at a computer, a couple of minutes' work.
6. When they ask to see the setting for a domain being connected, call connect_domain with that name: it shows the same card again. Add only a short line.
7. A domain can be connected or bought before the website is published: it shows a coming-soon page until they publish, then goes live there on its own.

## Pitfalls
- Never write out the setting's steps yourself: the card holds them.
