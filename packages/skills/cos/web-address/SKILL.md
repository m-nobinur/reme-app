# Web address

The website's own domain: one they have, finding or buying one, and connecting it. It is a task you lead. Say "domain" to the owner; "web address" only to explain what a domain is.

## When to use
- They mention a domain, their web address or publishing on their own name.
- The address card was answered, Connect now was pressed ("Help me set up my domain"), or the address task is under way.

## Procedure
1. Ask first. When they ask to connect or set up a domain and nothing names one yet (not their message, the open items note, nor business.email on a domain of its own), your whole reply is one short question: do they already have a domain, and if not, you can find one for them. Then offer_choices: Find me one ("No, help me get one") and I have one ("Yes, I have one"). NEVER assume they own one, NEVER guess or suggest a name here, and do not read connect-own until they say they have one. If the open items note already says they have none, skip the question and read buy-new. Done when they have said which.
2. When they say they have one but not its name, ask for it in one short question. Add the shape as an example only when it helps, such as yourbusiness.co.uk, never a name made from theirs.
3. The open items note says where the address stands, its next step and when to bring it up; code keeps that record, so go by it and never offer the domain on your own. The workspace's tasks hold its steps, a finished one included.
4. While an address task is under way, speak of its steps and offer nothing new.
5. When you bring it up, give the benefit in their terms (customers trust an address of their own, and it looks right on their quotes and cards) and that setting it up now means the website goes live on it the moment they publish, then ask whether they already have one.
6. When business.email in the facts is on a domain of its own, not free mail (gmail.com, outlook.com, yahoo.com, icloud.com and the like), that is the domain to offer: ask whether to use it.
7. When they have a domain but say the setting will have to wait (no login to hand, on their phone, later), put nothing off: the domain stays saved. Say so in your own words, with the why: their website goes live on its free address first so they can start sharing it, and the setting waits in their panel for whenever they're at a computer, a couple of minutes' work.
8. When they ask to see the setting for a domain being connected, call connect_domain with that name: it shows the same card again. Add only a short line.
9. A domain can be connected or bought before the website is published: it shows a coming-soon page until they publish, then goes live there on its own.

## Pitfalls
- Never write out the setting's steps yourself: the card holds them.
- Never bring the domain up twice in a sitting, and never after they put it off or said no; the open items note says when it may come back.
